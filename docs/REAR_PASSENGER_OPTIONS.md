# Video for passengers on ordinary trips

AA Tube shows video on the BMW's central display only while parked. For
watching during a drive, put the screen where **the driver can't see it**.
That is generally lawful (Texas §547.611 restricts displays *visible from the
operator's seat*), and it doesn't depend on Android Auto at all.

| Option | How | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tablet on a headrest mount** | Any Android tablet or iPad with the YouTube app, in a seat-back mount behind the front seats | Cheapest, simplest; official YouTube app, downloads for offline | Separate device and data plan or offline downloads |
| **Phone on a headrest mount** | A spare phone or the passenger's own phone | No extra hardware | Small screen |
| **Download for offline** | YouTube Premium downloads, or local files | No mobile data, no buffering | Premium for YouTube downloads |
| **Audio through the car** | Pair the tablet to the BMW's Bluetooth as a second audio device, or use passenger headphones | Shared sound | Bluetooth multi-device behaviour varies; headphones avoid driver distraction |
| **BMW rear-seat entertainment** | Factory/dealer option on some BMW models | Integrated | Generally not offered on the 3 Series; expensive |
| **Front passenger only** | Passenger watches on their own phone or tablet, angled away from the driver | Zero setup | Easy to drift into the driver's view, so keep it angled away |

Practical setup:

1. A headrest-post tablet mount (clamps on the two steel posts) holding a
   10–11" tablet.
2. A USB-C PD charger in a rear USB or 12 V outlet, if your car has one.
3. Downloads made at home on Wi-Fi before the trip.
4. Headphones for rear passengers, so the driver's audio stays on navigation.

None of these require AA Tube or Android Auto changes.
